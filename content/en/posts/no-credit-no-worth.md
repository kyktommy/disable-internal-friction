You spent three days fixing that system issue. Nobody said thank you. At the weekly meeting, your manager mentioned it in passing: "That bug's been resolved." Your name didn't come up.

Next time, another problem lands on your desk. Two days in, you can't crack it. A colleague solves it in an afternoon. Your manager posts in the group chat: "Thanks to so-and-so for the quick turnaround."

You stare at your screen. Your chest tightens. The voice appears: Am I even useful?

---

## The Game You Always Lose

Look at the structure. Solve it — nobody cares. Don't solve it — someone else proves you weren't needed.

You're playing a game where every roll of the dice is a loss. Win and there's no prize. Lose and you get docked. No rational person would keep playing a game like this. You keep playing because you've made "being seen" the only way to score.

The problem is the scoreboard. Not your ability.

---

## You're Measuring Yourself by Other People's Reactions

There's a hidden belief running inside you: whether what I do has value depends on whether anyone notices.

It's quiet. You'd never say it out loud. You don't even think you believe it. But every time you finish something, your eyes sweep the room — did anyone see? Did anyone mention it? If not, the deflation arrives. Like the task got done but its worth evaporated.

You've turned recognition into proof of value. Without someone stamping it, you're not sure it counts.

---

## Why "Someone Else Did It" Stings So Much

Someone solved the problem you couldn't. Objectively, this is a good thing. The problem's gone, the team benefits, things move forward.

The voice in your head doesn't do that math. It says: they could, you couldn't. You're surplus. If you weren't here, the outcome would be the same.

That voice is doing something specific — swapping "I didn't get this one task" for "I as a person am not good enough." A single task's outcome gets inflated into a verdict on your entire existence.

You are not that task. Your worth doesn't shift based on who gets credit for one fix.

---

## Credit Is the Most Fragile Fuel

People who run on credit look energetic. They jump at problems, rush to deliver, fight to be seen. When the tank is full, they move fast.

The problem is this fuel burns instantly. One round of being overlooked and the warning light flashes. One round of being outperformed and the engine stalls. You need constant affirmation just to keep going.

That's not drive. That's dependence.

The fuel that actually lasts doesn't come from applause. It comes from a quiet judgment: this was worth doing, so I did it. No other reason needed.

## What Adlerian Psychology Says

Adler would say you're fighting a war of **vertical relationships**. You've turned every interaction into a ranking — who's more capable, more useful, more worthy of attention. In this vertical world, someone else's success automatically equals your failure.

**Separation of tasks** cuts clean here: you solved the problem — that was your task, and it's done. Whether your manager publicly acknowledges it is your manager's task. How your colleagues evaluate you is their task. You've gathered everyone else's tasks onto your shoulders and used their outcomes to grade yourself. Of course you're exhausted. You're not doing one person's job. You're running emotional accounting for the entire company.

Adler's alternative is **social interest** — does what you do contribute to the community? If the system runs stable because of your three days of work, that's a contribution. No certificate required. The contribution itself is the meaning. Move your eyes from "who's watching me" to "where did my work go." The internal scorekeeper quiets down.

## What the Diamond Sutra and Heart Sutra Say

The Diamond Sutra says: 「應無所住而生其心」 — "Give rise to a mind that dwells nowhere." Where is your mind dwelling right now? On credit. On "did anyone notice." On "how do I compare to that person." Each dwelling is a nail pinning you to emotional turbulence. Someone sees your work, you're elated. No one does, you collapse. Your mind is yanked around by external reactions, swinging several times a day.

The sutra continues: 「凡所有相，皆是虛妄」 — "All appearances are illusory." Credit is an appearance. Being overlooked is an appearance. Someone else's success making you feel small — that's an appearance too. These feel real because the tightness in your chest is real, the heat in your face is real. But the reality of a sensation doesn't make the judgment real. You leapt from "no one thanked me" to "I have no value," and you skipped over a vast emptiness in between. Nothing lives in that gap. Your thoughts filled it with a story.

The Heart Sutra says: 「無智亦無得」 — "No wisdom and no attainment." Nothing is truly "gotten." Credit, recognition, a single word of praise — these things you thought would make you safe can vanish the next second. Security built on "getting" has an empty foundation.

## What the I Ching Says

**Hexagram 2: Kun — The Receptive (地)**

Kun is pure yin, all six lines yielding. The image of the earth. The earth carries everything and lets everything grow. It never asks for credit. Rivers run across it, forests root into it, cities rise on top of it. The earth has never said: "Look what I did."

The judgment reads: 「坤，元亨，利牝馬之貞」 — "The Receptive: supreme success, perseverance of a mare furthered." The mare's quality is gentle endurance — steady direction without display. Kun's power isn't in being seen. It's in continuing to carry.

The *Xiangzhuan* says: 「地勢坤，君子以厚德載物」 — "The earth's condition is receptive devotion. The superior person carries all things with generous virtue." The word "carry" is key here. All that unseen work you do — that's carrying. The system running smoothly, problems quietly resolved, the ground beneath the team staying level — you carried those things into being.

The earth doesn't stop growing things because nobody thanks it. Its value isn't in being admired. Its value is that everything stands on it, steady. So is yours.

## Try This

Next time you finish something and hear silence, ask yourself one question: what got better because I did this? Don't ask whether anyone noticed. Ask the work itself. If the answer is "something did," let that be your receipt. You don't need anyone else to confirm it.
