Internal friction is the drag you feel when part of you pulls against another part.

You want to do the work. You also want credit for it. You want to help your friend. You also notice they're a step ahead of you. You want peace. You also want to win.

Every ounce of that pulling burns energy. Energy that never reaches your life.

Most people try to push through. Grind harder. That just adds more pulling.

Faster way: change how you see three specific things. Each reframe removes a whole category of friction.

### Reframe 1: You're not working for yourself.

Every action is for something bigger. God. The universe. The future. Your family line. The craft itself. Pick whatever word works for you. The shift is that you stop being the main character.

When you're the main character, every task is a test. Did I do well? Did they notice? Was that good enough? The ego turns a simple email into a referendum on your worth.

That's friction.

When you're a contributor to something bigger, the email is just an email. You write it well because the thing deserves it. Praise lands lightly. So does criticism. You're free.

### Reframe 2: Nothing is against you.

Everything is a friend. The rude stranger. The rejected pitch. The colleague who got the promotion you wanted.

None of it is hostile to you.

Friction shows up as envy, defensiveness, resentment — the postures of a person under attack. But no one is attacking you. Their win is theirs. It didn't reach into your pocket and take something out.

The real switch: feel good when other people win. Not performatively. Actually.

When your friend gets the thing you wanted, can you be glad for them? The first few times it's hard. Then the muscle builds. One day you realize the world stopped being a zero-sum game you were losing.

### Reframe 3: Everything is fine.

Good outcomes are lessons. Bad outcomes are also lessons — they just come through the back door. A bad example is still an example.

Most of your friction comes from treating outcomes as verdicts. "This went well, so I'm good." "This went badly, so I'm bad." Stop ranking outcomes as judgments on your self. They become what they are.

Data.

The bad meeting teaches you something the good meeting couldn't. The failed project reveals a blind spot you didn't know you had. The person who hurt you showed you exactly what you don't want to become.

Nothing that happens is disqualifying. Nothing is final.

---

Three reframes. Use them together.

You're contributing to something bigger, so your ego isn't on the line. Nothing is against you, so you have nothing to defend. Everything is data, so nothing can disqualify you.

What's left is the doing. The doing, without friction, is fast.

## What Adlerian Psychology Says

Adler would say friction comes from the goal you're secretly chasing. If the goal is "be superior" or "avoid judgment," every interaction turns into a contest. Shift the goal to contribution — to the community, the task, something outside yourself — and the contest disappears.

Envy dies when you stop competing. Other people's wins become wins for the larger whole you're part of. Adler called this *Gemeinschaftsgefühl* — community feeling. He saw it as the antidote to the private, self-centered striving that produces most neurosis.

On "everything is fine" — this is the separation of tasks. The outcome belongs to the task. Your worth does not. You can do the work without bundling your identity into the result.

When you stop defending an image of yourself, you stop fighting the world.

## What the Diamond Sutra and Heart Sutra Say

The Diamond Sutra says a bodhisattva acts without attachment to the fruit of action. Do not dwell on the self, on others, on outcomes. "Every action is for something bigger" is one way of saying this. The actor dissolves into the act.

"Everything is a friend" — when there's no fixed self to defend, there's no one to attack it. The line between self and other softens. Their joy and your joy are not different currencies.

"Everything is fine" — form is emptiness, emptiness is form. Good and bad are labels the mind paints onto events. The events themselves just are.

The mind that doesn't label is the mind without friction.

## What the I Ching Says

The I Ching treats every situation as a passing configuration of yin and yang. Never fixed. Good fortune turns to bad. Bad fortune turns to good. Nothing is disqualifying because nothing is final. This is Hexagram 63 (既濟, After Completion) flipping into 64 (未濟, Before Completion). Over and over. You're never done. You're never ruined.

"Work for something bigger" is the teaching of Hexagram 15 (謙, Modesty). The one who takes themselves out of the center is supported by heaven, earth, and people alike.

"Everything is a friend" — the ten thousand things arise from the same source. Nothing is truly separate. Nothing is truly opposed.

The sage doesn't fight the river. The sage floats, and arrives.

## Try This

Pick one thing you have to do today — a task, a meeting, an email. Before you start, say quietly: "This isn't for me." Then do it well. See if the pressure lightens.
